# Cover Letter Craft

> Read by `cover-letter`. Companion file: `ai-fingerprint.md`.

---

## CL Format Rules

- Cover letter with resume: 1 page, 250-300 words, 3 paragraphs
- Full package: Resume + CL = 3 pages
- Never spill to a second page. If you're over 300 words, cut, don't shrink the font.

---

## Company Type Detection

Detect from the JD and company research, then adjust emphasis:

- **Product company:** Ship velocity, user impact, ownership. Reference the actual product.
- **Enterprise software / platform:** Customer outcomes, scale, reliability, integration surface.
- **Consulting / customer-facing:** Communication, ambiguity tolerance, breadth across stacks, stakeholder management.
- **Startup:** Range, autonomy, willingness to work outside your title, speed over polish.
- **Internal transfer:** You already know the systems and the vocabulary. Lead with what you've already delivered inside the company and what you want to do next. Skip the "why this company" paragraph — it's wasted space. Use it for "why this team, now."

---

## Standard Cover Letter (250-300 words, 3 paragraphs)

**P1 — HOOK:** Connect their product, platform, or problem to something you've actually built. State the position you're applying for. Open with a specific reference to their work, not a generic opener. Keep jargon low; a recruiter often reads first.

**P2 — EVIDENCE:** 2-3 achievements translated into business value. Max 3-4 quantified claims. Mirror the JD's terms. Frame as outcomes delivered, not tasks performed.

**P3 — CLOSING:** Forward-looking value plus an active call to action. If you're pivoting domains, frame what the new role enables rather than what your current one lacks.

---

## Universal CL Rules

- Open with a specific reference to their work — avoid generic openers like "I am writing to express my interest"
- Add narrative context the resume cannot — motivation, "why this company," where you want to go
- Limit quantified claims to 3-5 per letter
- Weave credentials into body paragraphs; never dump them in the closing
- Active call to action in the closing, not a passive "Thank you for your consideration"
- If pivoting domains: lead with transferable methodology in P1, not apologetic framing

---

## Jargon Calibration

Assume a recruiter or HR screener reads first and a hiring manager reads second. Write so the first reader isn't lost and the second reader isn't bored. Use the JD's own vocabulary rather than your team's internal names for things.

---

## Package Reading Rules

- The resume must stand alone — many hiring managers never read the cover letter
- The CL deepens, it does not introduce. Every major CL claim should be traceable to a resume bullet.
- No contradictions between documents
- Resume + CL = 3 pages

---

## CL Hook Verification (MANDATORY)

Before presenting any CL draft to the user, web-search and verify every external reference:
- Product name, feature, or technology claim → confirm it exists and you've described it correctly
- Company news, funding, launch, or strategy claim → confirm it's accurate and current
- Named team, org, or person → confirm the name and role

**If verified:** Note the source URL in `targets/<slug>/brief.md`.
**If unverified:** Flag as **"UNVERIFIED — please confirm"** in the draft. Never guess product names or company details. Getting a product name wrong in the first sentence is fatal.

---

## CL Anti-Patterns

- No generic opener ("I am writing to express my interest...")
- No defensive framing ("Despite my background in...")
- No credential dump in the closing paragraph
- No repeating resume bullets verbatim — the CL deepens, it doesn't duplicate
- No flattery without substance ("I have long admired your innovative culture")
- Limit quantified claims to 3-5
- **Em-dashes:** Max 2 per document. Cover letters are prose-heavy and em-dashes compound quickly. Use commas for parenthetical asides, colons for elaborations, periods for new sentences.
